When Your System Fails in Savannah

Heating and cooling failures rarely wait for a convenient day, which is why we offer prompt, flexible scheduling for most situations. Arrival in the area is typically within a day for urgent calls, and a dead AC in a heat wave or a furnace out on a freezing night is the kind of call we move to the front of the line.

The emergency calls we answer usually follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's an AC blowing warm air, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil iced over. On the heating side, it's a furnace that ignites and drops out. And any time of year, it's breakers that trip every cycle, clogged condensate drains overflowing into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that stop responding. Since our trucks are stocked with capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, most of these are wrapped up in one visit, with the price agreed before any work begins.

Two common situations call for quick action from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or gives off a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and give us a call. Those few minutes can prevent a far bigger repair.

Keeping Your Savannah HVAC System Running Longer

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

How old is your system? That number says a lot about what comes next. With regular service, most air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that range and also shows higher bills, uneven temperatures, or repeated repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement when the first heat wave hits.

The sooner you call about a comfort problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A weak capacitor costs little to replace, yet that same capacitor, ignored through a full season, can take out the compressor and force a full system replacement. Save our number on your phone today so help is one call away the moment something sounds or feels wrong in your home. Early intervention saves time, money, and miserable nights.
